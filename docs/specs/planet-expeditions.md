# Planet Expeditions

The eight planets each offer three freely selectable missions. Every mission introduces three required concepts through three clue cards and three age-banded questions, then reinforces a concept through an evidence, classification, or discrete experiment activity. A fourth card offers optional deeper exploration. Bonus worlds and the Technology Lab keep their existing discovery flows.

The authored curriculum contains 24 missions, 96 cards, 72 questions with three age variants (216 core variants), 72 separately authored transfer questions, and 24 activities. Ages 4–6 use spoken recognition and two-choice quizzes; ages 7–9 compare concrete clues; ages 10–12 interpret evidence and data. Each concept and question links to a reviewed source independently of its image credit.

## Mission and passport flow

Mission Control offers a brief, clue 1 and its question, clue 2 and its question, clue 3, the science activity, and the final question. Every activity uses focusable buttons. Classification works through item tasks; experiments preview the selected setting's authored visual and text outcome before the player checks the model. Diagrams simplify sizes and distances and do not invent quantitative physics.

Hints and retries are unlimited and earn the same visible reward. The final correct answer commits mission completion and the planet stamp before the feedback screen. Replaying a completed mission cannot duplicate its reward. Three separate mission badges show deeper exploration of each planet; completing all 24 celebrates the planetary expedition.

## Native movies

Each planet has an optional 60-second native movie with local H.264 footage, three caption/narration segments, a transcript, and two questions at 20 and 40 seconds. Raw NASA visuals and existing credited photographs are transformed through the checked-in regeneration script. Exact source filenames, contributors, transformations, and credits accompany the media. The game does not request an online player or network access for these lessons.

The core owns checkpoint eligibility. Boundary and periodic AVPlayer callbacks find the earliest unanswered checkpoint. Forward seeks cannot bypass it; replay shows the preceding clue again; feedback and repeated callbacks cannot advance a checkpoint twice. The player pauses on game pause, scene inactivity, interruption, or exit. Loading a save never autoplays. Missing or unreadable media offers illustrated clues with the same questions and explanations.

## Saved progress and review

Schema 3 adds mission/video completion ledgers, review records keyed by concept and Explorer Mode, and one active learning cursor. Version 1/2 destination stamps and legacy scores survive migration, while new mission badges start unearned. Starting another adventure replaces the active cursor. Returning to Worlds retains it; explicit Resume restores cards, questions, activities, feedback, or paused video. Invalidated content revisions restart the current adventure while preserving earned discoveries.

Concept reviews are scheduled once per completed encounter. First encounters are due the next day; later independent due encounters advance through 1/2/4/8/16-day intervals. Supported encounters invite another next-day review. Same-day replays do not advance retention records. Worlds offers an optional two-question memory adventure using separately authored transfer prompts, without notifications or streak targets.

The existing ordered save queue and recovery controls remain in use. Television logs remain below the 256 KiB bound; playback saves a compact cursor rather than a tick history.

## Accessibility and delivery

VoiceOver suppresses automatic speech. Narration is optional, captions and transcripts convey video content, and reduced motion removes custom transitions. Touch, Siri Remote, and native focus navigation reach every required action. Active adventures retain their starting Explorer Mode.

Content, state, migration, and resource tests accompany iPhone and television UI tests. CI checks formatting, repository and media integrity, both simulator builds, and both input flows. Xcode Cloud hydrates Git LFS assets before its existing archive/TestFlight pipeline.

## Offline planet discovery films

The eight bundled films are silent 60-second educational montages: 20 seconds of
NASA footage or visualization, followed by two 20-second credited image segments
with a gentle camera zoom. The app supplies original captions, three age-specific
transcripts, optional Apple speech narration, and pause-and-quiz prompts at 20 and
40 seconds. No source soundtrack, stock music, YouTube extraction, advertisement,
or network playback is included. Narration is provided by the device's installed
speech voice; the captions and read-the-story transcript always remain available.

Each film's complete institutional credit appears in the app and in the accepted
asset manifest. The image credits in the table above apply to those images reused
inside the montage. Kevin M. Gill's Jupiter images are attributed under the CC-BY
license stated by [NASA's Jupiter Marble page](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/jupiter-marble/)
and [Great Red Spot page](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/jupiters-great-red-spot-revealed/);
those sources do not specify a Creative Commons version, so the registry preserves
that distinction. The two Europa processing images retain their stated CC BY 3.0
credits and are not used in the eight planet films. Source images have been resized
and compressed; movie derivatives crop, loop, scale, concatenate, and zoom the
reviewed media. NASA and the credited collaborators do not endorse the game.

The films are 1280×720, 30 fps, H.264 Main Level 3.1 in MP4 containers. Together they
occupy 7.76 MiB, below the 100 MiB media budget, and are tracked with Git LFS.
`scripts/video_media_sources.json` records the exact official download URLs,
source SHA-256 digests, image digests, output digests, credits, crops and review
notes. Regenerate using FFmpeg 7.1 with libx264:

```sh
python3 scripts/generate_learning_videos.py --ffmpeg /path/to/ffmpeg
```

The script verifies source digests, stores raw downloads outside the repository,
and decodes every output frame before replacing a bundled film. The pinned output
digests were produced with FFmpeg 7.1; a different encoder version may produce
different bytes and requires an updated reviewed output record. Only the generated
movies, source records, and original generation script are committed.
